Silent aim

Silent aim changes the angle the server receives, not the angle you look at. Your camera, your F5 body and your walk direction stay exactly where you put them.

What goes out on the wire

The rotation is written onto your outgoing movement packet. When vanilla would send no packet at all — you are standing still and not turning — the client sends a single look packet with the aim angle, so a mace held over a target still reaches the server. An angle the server already has is never repeated: a look packet that never changes is a tell.

What never happens

The player entity is never rotated on the client. That is why nothing shows in F5, why an elytra flight does not bend, and why your character keeps walking the way your keyboard says instead of steering toward the target. It also means other mods reading your body rotation see your own look.

What the server still knows

Combat has to be checked against a direction. The server sees the aim angle on the packet — it has to, otherwise the hit would be rejected as a direction mismatch. Silent aim hides the rotation from your screen and your body, not from the packet. That is the honest limit of every silent aim.

Turn it on in Kill Aura → Aim → Silent Aim, Auto Mace → Aim → Silent Aim, or in the standalone Silent Aura module. With Aim off, those auras hit with the angle you already have.

Aim, Aim Assist and smoothing

Three different things, named almost the same. Picking the wrong one is the most common reason a module “feels off”.

Aim (visible)

Turns your real camera onto the target. You see it, F5 shows it, and your movement follows the new look. This is what most clients call a normal aimbot. Inside Kill Aura and Auto Mace this is the Aim toggle with Silent Aim off.

Silent aim

The same target selection, delivered on the packet only. Nothing on your screen changes. Use this when you still want to steer your own movement or keep an eye on a second player.

Aim Assist & Smooth

Its own module, and the smooth option inside the auras. Speed runs from 50% to 100%: 100% is the same as 40° vertical and 40° horizontal per tick, 50% is the same as 20° and 20°. Lower is slower and more human; higher snaps. Smoothing only exists while Smooth is selected — the plain mode shows no sliders at all.

Kill Aura's aim and its silent aim use the same smoothing system: if Smooth is on, both modes ease in at the speed you picked instead of snapping 180° in one tick.

Silent swap

A silent swap moves an item on the server while your hotbar keeps showing what you were holding. The click is an ordinary container click on window 0, which is always open, so no inventory screen is opened and nothing is drawn.

Where it is used

Auto Mace → Silent Swap swaps the mace in and out of the hand without moving your hotbar. Silent Auto Totem and Totem Predict → Silent place a totem in the offhand the same way. Silent Take Off and Silent Requip change your chest slot with three container clicks.

The visible versions

With the silent switch off, the module selects the hotbar slot first — your hand really changes — and then performs the swap. You will see the item appear, which is the point: some players prefer knowing what their character is holding.

Why clicks and not key presses

A key press only exists on your keyboard. A container click is a packet the server already accepts from a player with an inventory open, it carries its own window id, and it needs no screen on your side. That is what makes it both fast and quiet.

Auto Mace

The mace sequence: fall past three blocks, aim once, swap, hit, swap back. Every delay below is a real option, not a hidden constant.

  • Fall gate. The module only arms once you have fallen three blocks or more. Under three blocks a mace hit is a normal hit and the swap is wasted.
  • Range. Aim and swap only happen when the target is inside four blocks. Past that the module holds still instead of aiming at something you cannot reach.
  • Aim. With Aim on, Silent Aim appears. Silent sends the angle on the packet; visible turns your camera. Both accept the Smooth system above.
  • Silent Swap. The server-side slot changes, the hotbar does not. Your hand keeps the item you were holding while the hit lands with the mace.
  • Reaction. The cooldown is zero by default, and the attack timing matches the triggerbot: the hit goes out on the first tick the cooldown allows, not on a fixed delay.
  • Take Off / Requip. Takeoff Elytra puts a chestplate on (netherite, then any) so the elytra comes off without leaving the slot empty; Requip puts the elytra back after the hit, and Silent Requip does it without touching the hotbar.

The pearl catch modules were removed from the Mace category in this build. Auto Pearl Catch is back as an empty entry for the rewrite.

Totems

Three modules cover the same idea at different moments: before the damage, on the pop, and when you open your own inventory.

Totem Predict

When your health drops below Minimum Health (1–20, the same scale as the health bar) it moves a totem into your offhand. Silent keeps your hotbar and camera still. Delay waits a rolled 0–500 ms before the swap; off means the swap happens on the tick the threshold is crossed. Whitelist names the items allowed to stay in the offhand, so a shield or a gapple is never silently thrown out.

Silent Auto Totem

The on-pop version. It waits for the offhand to be empty, waits its open delay, sends one swap click and closes the handler again. Hotbar Totem fills a hotbar slot instead, and the same whitelist protects that slot.

Auto Totem

The full inventory routine, with Mode for Crystal or SMP and a measure list that decides how much danger it is willing to ignore. Use it when you want the client to manage the whole inventory shuffle.

Totem Predict reacts to the health you have now, not to damage that has been sent but not applied yet. It is a preparation module: it makes sure a totem is already in the offhand when the next hit lands.

Kill Aura, hitbox and reach

Kill Aura has three ways to decide when to click, and the range is deliberately capped at a number a server will accept.

  • Normal. Attacks when the vanilla cooldown is full, at your configured range.
  • CPS. Ignores the cooldown and clicks on a schedule between Min CPS and Max CPS, anywhere from 1 to 20.
  • Triggerbot. Only attacks the moment the cooldown is fully gone — no early clicks, no wasted swings.
  • Range. Up to 3.00 blocks, not 2.95. That is the number the server measures against, with a little room for movement between your packet and its check.
  • Hitbox Expand. Widens the target box for your own selection. With the switch off, the size slider disappears — a value that is not in use should not be editable.
  • Villagers stay safe. Kill Aura never targets them, whatever the target filter says.

Extended reach was removed from Kill Aura. A hit distance the server does not believe is rejected silently, which looks like a module that does nothing.

Backtrack

Backtrack keeps a player's hitbox where it was a moment ago, so you can hit a position they have already left. The window runs from 0 to 5000 ms.

Low window (50–250 ms)

Matches ordinary latency. It feels like the game is being fair to you rather than like a feature, which is exactly why it is the safest setting on a server you care about.

High window (1000 ms and up)

You can hit someone who is visibly gone. It is also the setting most likely to look impossible in a recording: your swing lands where nobody is standing. Use it knowingly.

Packet hygiene

Most “the module threw my pearl sideways” reports are not aim bugs. They are two packets disagreeing about which way you were looking.

  • Look and use travel together. An item-use packet carries the direction the item goes in. Before it leaves, the client makes sure the server's last known rotation is the same angle, so a wind charge and the movement packet that follows cannot disagree.
  • No swings while eating. A swing sent while you are using an item releases the food first. That swing is held back until the use ends.
  • No double clicks. A rotation that has already been sent is not sent again, and a hit is only accepted with an angle the server has actually received.
  • Your own hooks stay out of it. The modules that emit their own clicks and rotations run against the same guard, so a silent aim and a pearl catch cannot overwrite each other's angle mid-throw.

When it feels wrong

My camera still turns by itself

A visible aim is on somewhere: Aim without Silent Aim, or Aim Assist with smoothing. Silent aim never touches the camera, so if the view moves, look for the module that asked for it.

I drift while walking straight

A module is writing the rotation outside the packet path. Silent aim cannot do that by design; if you see it, note the module and send it in. The report is worth more than a guess.

A throw goes the wrong way

Check whether a second module is holding a silent aim at that moment. Two owners, one angle: the sanitizer resolves it, but the module that started last wins. Turn one off and compare.

A module does nothing

Three usual causes: the module is enabled but no license is active on this PC, the gamemode filter hides it (Mace modules need the Mace tag), or the option it depends on is off and its slider is hidden. Open the module and check the toggles above the slider.

Nothing shows in F5

That is the intended result. A silent rotation is never applied to your body, so there is nothing for F5 to show.

The GUI is blurry behind the panels

That is the designed background: the world is blurred rather than covered by a black sheet. If it looks wrong, check that the game is not running with a mod that replaces the GUI blur pass.
